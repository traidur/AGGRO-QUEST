# QUEST — Inspirations

Existing games and mechanics worth borrowing/stealing from, organized by which QUEST subsystem they'd feed. Not canon — a reference list to raid when a system needs a concrete precedent. Ethos filter for everything here: **no dice, no visible stick, all carrot, no visible movement tax.**

## Market Row / Claim phase contention

- **Star Realms / Ascension** — shared center row of purchasable cards, refilled on buy, contested purely by turn order. Near 1:1 precedent for the Market Row + one-claim-per-round structure.
- **Puerto Rico** — rotating role/priority selector each round, with a small compensation bonus for whoever picks first. Candidate answer to `OPEN_QUESTIONS.md` #1 (Claim-phase fairness): instead of a fixed clockwise pass, give whoever went last this round soft priority next round so no one gets structurally starved at a hot node.

## Deck culling / leveling

- **Great Western Trail** — players loop a track and pay to permanently discard weak starting cards from their deck, thinning it so good cards draw more often. Near-exact precedent for QUEST's "Cull on level-up," just decoupled from a physical loop track. Worth studying how GWT paces the culling cost curve.

## Deterministic combat

- **Res Arcana** — zero dice, pure resource-conversion combat/scoring. Study for how it avoids decisions going stale despite full determinism.
- **Gloomhaven**'s attack-modifier deck (small, bounded variance layered on fixed base numbers) is the standard answer to `OPEN_QUESTIONS.md` #3 (solved-hand risk) if OTK math ever feels too solvable — not a recommendation to adopt, since it's a soft dice-replacement that may read as stick-adjacent, but the pattern worth knowing.

## Durability (gear wear, town-only repair)

- **Monster Hunter** — weapon sharpness degrades over a hunt, restored at the hub between hunts, never mid-field. Near-exact video-game precedent for the Durability pillar; good one to cite when explaining the pillar to players since it's already in their mental model.

## Bag Tetris (shared consumable/loot slots)

- **Diablo / Path of Exile** — shared inventory grid where potions and loot compete for the same space. The exact tension QUEST is going for.
- **Darkest Dungeon**'s stagecoach provisioning — buy torches/food/bandages before an expedition; they physically occupy inventory slots loot would otherwise fill. Closest tabletop-shaped analog to the specific squeeze.

## Decaying Bounties

Weakest board-game ancestry of any QUEST system — it's really modeled on MMO systems (WoW daily/weekly bonus objectives, Guild Wars 2's event reward scaling), which may mean it's QUEST's most novel contribution. Tabletop-adjacent idea worth stealing:

- **Merchants & Marauders / Xia: Legends of a Drift System** — expiration lives on the individual contract card, not a shared game-wide clock. Consistent with "no *visible* stick": nothing forces the pace except a card the player chose to accept.

## No movement tax

Not borrowed from one game — it's how worker-placement Eurogames already work (**Stone Age**, **Lords of Waterdeep**, **Caverna**): movement/placement is free, and the entire "cost" is opportunity cost from someone else taking the spot first. The Claim phase is already doing all the tax-enforcement work; no separate movement cost lever needed.
