I'm designing a board game called QUEST — a prequel/companion to an existing project called AGGRO (a Slay-the-Spire-x-WoW card-based deckbuilder raid-boss game). QUEST reuses AGGRO's classes and card economy but is a logistical engine-builder simulating MMO questing/farming/looting, not raid combat. You've reviewed this project before (puzzle quality, then the Cleric fix and diagnostic suite) — this is a full state-of-the-game update covering everything since, because a lot has changed. Give me your honest, independent read on whatever stands out; I'm not asking a narrow question this time, just want your two cents on where this stands.

## The core system, unchanged

Combat is a fast "toll check" gating access to loot — the real game is a logistics loop (trip planning, bag space, loot decay, how many fights before heading back to town), not the point itself. **6-card unique deck, 4-card hand, exactly 3 rounds per pull**, one card played per round (draw 4, sequence 3, one card deliberately left unplayed), no Energy costs, no dice anywhere except which 4-of-6 hand you draw. Deck fully resets every pull; only raw HP carries forward across a trip. Exact-enumeration solver, not Monte Carlo — for every possible hand, it finds the provably optimal line.

## What's changed since the last check-in

**A real, embarrassing miscommunication got found and fixed.** Warrior's Stance mechanic was supposed to be locked once per pull (no flip, ever) — but a mid-conversation context compaction silently dropped that decision before it was ever written into the code, and every subsequent balance pass (a full Vanguard Blade/Shield rework, stance-balance tuning) ran against the *old*, flip-enabled solver while everyone involved believed no-flip was already locked. Caught only because the user pushed back hard on a stance-balance number that didn't match their own recollection. Re-verified the entire Warrior kit against the corrected rule once found — the damage floor/ceiling and single-pull win rates were completely unaffected (the flip never mattered for *whether* a hand could win, only for HP-preservation margins), but the Guardian/Champion stance-balance claim from before the fix was flatly wrong and had to be recomputed from scratch. The corrected, final shape: Guardian wins clearly against weak mobs, Champion against strong ones, kept deliberately rather than flattened toward artificial 50/50 — it reads as a genuine mob-matchup decision, not noise.

**Execute (Warrior's finisher) is now stance-agnostic** — 6 dmg if the mob is ≤50% HP, in either stance, rather than Champion-only. Closed a "Guardian has no reason to ever finish a fight" gap the no-flip fix exposed.

**Cleric's Fiery Fortitude got a real implementation bug, caught by hand-tracing.** Its card text ("+2 Max HP, 2 heal") was implemented as two separately-stacking effects — the max-HP buff granting its own instant +2 current HP *on top of* the card's already-separate heal stat — which silently doubled the card's real healing output and reopened Cleric's previously-fixed "cannot die" equilibrium bug on two specific mobs. The fix: the max-HP buff raises the ceiling only, doesn't grant instant HP of its own; the existing heal stat is what actually heals, just no longer wasted above the old cap. Confirmed via a controlled test that a flat, static HP increase (no mechanic involved at all) breaks the same equilibrium the same way — this was never really about the card, it's that those two mobs were tuned with ~zero margin the first time this bug was found and fixed, and any new source of Cleric healing throughput, however small, threatens that margin again.

**Wizard and Cleric are now both HP=14** (were 10 and 12). Verified via a full sweep (survivability scales almost perfectly linearly with HP, ~+0.25 wins per +1 HP, no diminishing returns found across a wide range) and re-checked clean on equilibrium at the new value with the corrected Fiery Fortitude fix. Lands both classes at essentially exact mixed-roster parity with Warrior.

**A fourth class, Paladin, is now fully built** — first real test of whether a "how to balance a new class" process doc (written after Warrior/Wizard/Cleric, generalized from their experience) actually holds up on a class it wasn't written from. AGGRO's real Paladin kit was pulled and spot-checked directly against the source CSV/design doc rather than trusted from a research summary alone (one real discrepancy found and set aside: a card's derived numeric columns didn't match its own rules text). Paladin's real mechanic (Holy Seals — a Virtue becomes an ongoing Active passive once played) got redesigned mid-build from a 3-card structure (two Virtue-Attacks + a shared Invocation card) into two self-contained cards, "Invocation of Sanctuary" / "Invocation of Grace," each simultaneously a payoff for STRIKE cards already played *and* a setup for STRIKE cards played after — but **only the first Invocation played each pull gets any bonus at all; the second is flat base damage only.** That exact boundary condition (first vs. second, not just "is one active") had to be stated explicitly after an early draft silently double-counted it, same root-cause bug class as the Fiery Fortitude issue above.

Paladin's tuning found something the existing floor/ceiling diagnostic couldn't see on its own: the floor number looked fine (8, close to the other three classes' 8-10) while a full 8 of 15 possible hands (53%) were *mathematically incapable* of killing two specific mobs regardless of play — the floor is one worst-case hand, not a distribution. Added a new diagnostic for this (count of hands that can't possibly win against a given mob) as a result. After iteration, Paladin landed at 3.03 mixed-roster wins against Warrior's 3.02 — essentially an exact tie — and is now competitive (top-two of four classes) on every single mob in the roster, not just in aggregate.

**The flee/timeout reward question got resolved, not just measured.** Whether letting a mob's clock run out should pay anything was undecided for most of this project (there's a standing "flee preference" diagnostic that measures how often a HP-maximizing line doesn't kill the mob, explicitly built as measurement-only because the answer depended on this). Landed on: **a timeout should be worth approximately nothing, unless it's the only thing that kept the hero alive.** Tested two alternatives and rejected both: pricing loot big enough to make voluntary timeouts irrational on their own would require a kill to be worth roughly a third to half of a class's max HP (absurd); a round-based loot-decay curve (faster kills paid disproportionately more) was rejected because the multiplier needed to fix one cross-class gap this way (~3.5x) amounts to a universal speed tax that punishes Warrior for correctly playing its own sustain-grind identity, which finishes in round 3 about 88% of the time.

**A full interactive playtest tool now exists** — a human can actually draw a hand and play a pull, not just read simulator output. Small step-by-step engine (separate from, but numerically synced with, the exhaustive solvers), a terminal front end, and a Flask web app with drag-and-drop card play (modeled on AGGRO's own web UI, though it turned out AGGRO's isn't a "stage a plan, execute later" system either — dragging a card just submits it immediately, same as a button click, only the gesture differs). Cards show their mechanic role live in the UI now too (e.g. "Spellweave payoff -- ACTIVE this round" vs. "(not armed)"), not just raw numbers — this was a real, specifically-requested usability gap, not a nice-to-have. All four classes are wired into it now, including Paladin's brand-new Invocation mechanic, cross-checked against the exhaustive solvers (100+ scripted hands per class, exact HP/outcome match required).

## Still open, not solved

- **No Food/Winded/rest-cycle model exists yet.** Every survivability number in this whole project is "pulls before HP≤0," a proxy for "pulls before being forced back to town," not the real macro-loop mechanic — nothing enforces the actual gate yet.
- **No chain-bonus math exists.** If pulls chaining together are meant to produce an escalating reward (proposed, not built), a class with structurally shorter chains needs something quantified to compensate — total value per trip is what should be equalized, not raw pull-count survived, and nothing computes that yet.
- Paladin's one remaining soft spot: single-pull win rate on two specific mobs (Sentinel, Champion) sits a few points below the other three classes — not broken, just the one place left that isn't fully to parity.
