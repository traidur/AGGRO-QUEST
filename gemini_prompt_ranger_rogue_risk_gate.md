Quick context if this is a fresh conversation: QUEST is a board game (prequel to a project called AGGRO) built around a small, exact-enumeration combat model — 6-card unique deck per class, 4-card hand, exactly 3 rounds per pull, no dice except which hand you draw. On top of that sits a Town/Bag/Quest/Gold macro loop (`macro_sim.py`): a hero chains pulls into trips, trips into quest turn-ins, and a risk policy decides whether a given pull is safe enough to attempt. Nine classes exist and are locked at Level 1. I'm currently building a Level-2 upgrade pass per class, and pricing those upgrades in Gold is what led to this whole investigation.

## How we actually got here — this took a long, winding path, not a clean diagnosis

**1. Started by asking a pricing question.** To price Level-2 upgrades in Gold, I wanted to know how much Gold a hero would typically have by the time they'd hit a provisional Level-2 XP threshold (24 XP). Measured it for all 9 classes. Paladin averaged 23.8 Gold at that point; Ranger averaged only 15.3 — worst in the roster, and its median "Gold left after buying the one locked Bag Upgrade (16G)" was effectively zero.

**2. Checked whether that gap was even real, not just noise.** 95% confidence intervals across 300 trials came back completely non-overlapping (Paladin [2.134, 2.161] quests/trip vs. Ranger [1.756, 1.781]) — genuinely real, not sampling noise.

**3. Went looking for which of our "normal" stats explains it.** We already had a whole family of diagnostics from earlier balance work: deaths/run, nothing-tier decay %, gambles-taken, avg lethal-hand-fraction, and a "clean" defense-floor sweep (lethal-hand-fraction at fixed HP checkpoints, independent of the risk policy). Correlated all of them against the Gold gap across all 9 classes. The strongest correlate by far was `avg_quests_per_trip` (Pearson r = +0.984) — everything else, including the metrics we'd normally reach for first (deaths/run, decay%), correlated weakly (r between -0.35 and -0.52).

**4. Went hunting for *why* Paladin's quests/trip is so high specifically.** This is the part that took the longest and mostly failed, which is itself informative:
   - Kill-round timing (which round the optimal line actually kills in): Paladin is unremarkable, nearly identical to Cleric and Runecaster.
   - Raw per-round HP economy (net HP lost per round, block absorbed per round): Paladin looked like the *worst* in the whole 9-class roster at first (1.57 HP lost/round, worst mitigation at 1.49) — a real, per-mob-verified number, not a bug.
   - That number turned out to be misleading. Paladin also finishes fights in fewer total rounds than most other classes, which shrinks the denominator in a "per round" rate. Once we built a proper tool (`per_round_economy` / `per_round_economy_table`, now a permanent function) that normalizes total per-pull HP cost against each class's own max HP instead of raw HP, Paladin and Ranger came out **tied at 21.6%** of max HP lost per pull. So per-pull combat efficiency, properly normalized, is not the answer — the two classes at opposite ends of the Gold ranking cost the same fraction of their own HP pool per pull.
   - Win rate, wins/pull, and "flee preference" (a measurement of how often an HP-preserving non-kill line beats the best kill line) were all checked too. None distinguished Paladin from the middle of the pack.

**5. The actual mechanism, found by tracing real trips instead of static per-pull averages.** Instrumented `run_one_trip` to record how each trip actually ends: fully completing both active quests without ever being stopped, being cut short by the risk policy, or dying. Paladin completes a full trip cleanly 10.9% of the time; Necromancer 9.3%; **Ranger only 0.8%** — a ~14x gap. Ranger's trips also get cut short earlier on average (6.61 pulls vs. Paladin's 7.82) when they do get stopped.

   The mechanism: the risk policy runs at two tolerances — a fixed `RISK_TOLERANCE_BASE = 0.0` (zero risk tolerated) for ordinary pulls, and a higher `RISK_TOLERANCE` (currently 15%, and separately validated via a 3-persona sweep at 5%/15%/30%) that only applies when a pull would complete a quest outright. This project already knew, from earlier balance work, that Rogue and Ranger are the *only* two classes in the roster whose worst-case lethal-hand-fraction goes above 0% already at 50% HP (a full HP-tier earlier than the clean 0.0% floor every other class holds down to 33%) — Rogue from a pure card-count gap (one specific hand has zero Block anywhere in it), Ranger from a mob-type-specific one (its evasion-based defense is fully neutralized against the one ranged mob in the roster). We confirmed directly that **100% of risk-stops in real trips fire on the fixed, persona-independent 0.0% gate**, never the persona-varied one — which also explains a separate earlier puzzle: running the whole roster through the aggressive persona (30% tolerance on quest-completing pulls) barely moved Ranger's numbers at all, because that knob doesn't touch the part of the policy that's actually gating it.

**6. Tested whether raising the base tolerance uniformly (removing the special "last pull" exemption entirely, same tolerance for every pull) is a real lever.** It is, but it's blunt: at a uniform 15% tolerance, Ranger's Gold-at-24XP jumps from 14.5 to 17.4 (real, meaningful), and the whole roster's spread (max minus min class) tightens from 9.3 to 5.9 — but Paladin's own number actually drops slightly (23.8 to 23.3), because it wasn't gated in the first place and now occasionally gambles into a loss it would previously have avoided by retreating. Pushing further to 30% overshoots — even Ranger's own gain partially reverses as death/setback costs start outweighing the throughput benefit.

## What this means for overall balance, and my own read on it

This isn't a new problem — the underlying combat gap (Rogue's card-count floor, Ranger's evasion-vs-Scout gap) was already found and root-caused weeks ago, with four candidate fixes written down and none chosen. What's new is that this same root cause now has *three* independent, confirmed symptoms instead of one: elevated deaths/run (though that one turned out to be threshold-sensitive — Rogue's death-rate outlier status doesn't hold up under a more aggressive risk persona, while Ranger's does), worse decay-tier/Gold outcomes (robust across all three tested personas), and now this quests/trip and Gold-at-Level-2 gap (also robust, and now mechanistically explained down to which exact line of policy code fires).

My honest opinion: yes, this is worth fixing, and soon — not because the gap is catastrophic in absolute terms (Ranger still profits, just visibly slower than everyone else), but because we're about to start pricing Level-2 upgrades in Gold for every class, and pricing them fairly against a roster where one class structurally earns ~35% less Gold by the same XP checkpoint bakes an avoidable unfairness into every subsequent balance decision. I'd lean toward the surgical fix over the blunt one: a small, real Block rider on the specific card(s) diagnosed as the actual gap (one of Rogue's four "pure offense" cards; the Ranger card whose only current defense is an evasion tag that Scout ignores), rather than raising the risk policy's base tolerance roster-wide. The uniform-tolerance lever demonstrably works, but it taxes classes that were never part of the problem (Paladin loses ground) to partially help the ones that are, and it doesn't even fully close the gap. A raised HP floor is the other commonly-reached-for lever, but this project's own balance guide already rules it out here on principle: HP is the right fix when a class's kit produces good variance and economy on a pool that's simply too small, and the wrong fix when the real problem is a specific card-composition or mechanic gap — which this clearly is.

**Question for you:** does this reasoning hold up, or is there a category of fix or a piece of evidence we're missing? Specifically — is "pure per-pull economy, fully normalized, is provably tied between the best and worst class in the roster, and the entire gap is explained by a binary policy gate reacting to a nonzero-vs-zero lethal-hand-fraction distinction" a satisfying, complete explanation, or does it smell like there's a second layer underneath it we haven't found yet the same way the first "per-round economy" theory turned out to be an artifact?

## UPDATE: both classes fixed, tested, and locked (still 2026-08-19)

Didn't wait on an answer to the question above — kept digging ourselves, found the actual mechanism cleanly, and shipped real fixes for both classes. Full story below, since the path there matters as much as the numbers.

**First, a real methodology bug caught and fixed.** Every defense-floor number up to this point had been read off round-number HP checkpoints (100%/50%/33%/20%/10% of max HP). That checkpoint style turned out to be a bug already found and fixed once before, documented in `defense_floor_sweep`'s own docstring — an earlier version of the exact same tool swept those same fractional checkpoints and got corrected to an exhaustive per-integer-HP sweep instead, because most of those checkpoints aren't even real HP values a hero could have (50% of Paladin's 17 HP is 8.5). Despite the underlying tool already being fixed, the checkpoint style got reintroduced purely in how results were *displayed* this session. Re-ran everything against the full, unsampled sweep. The real, best single-number predictor turned out to be **first_crack_HP** — the highest raw HP (not a %) at which any mob first produces a nonzero lethal-hand-fraction for a class. Correlates at r=-0.924 against quests/trip and r=-0.889 against Gold@24XP across the full 9-class roster, the strongest structural predictor found in the whole investigation.

**Traced it down to an even more concrete, tabletop-legible number: the actual HP at which a real trip is forced to eat its one starting Food.** Instrumented the real trip loop (matching `run_one_trip` exactly, including the consumable-retry step) to log this directly across 1000 trials per class. Paladin eats its Food at HP=3.40 on average; Ranger at HP=4.58; Rogue at HP=4.73. Both classes need that one Food in essentially every single trip (1000/1000 for all three), so the entire game comes down to whether a *second* scare arrives before the trip would have finished anyway — and a ~1.2 HP head start on the first scare turns into an 89.7%-vs-99.6%-forced-to-stop-short gap in practice (Paladin vs. Ranger).

**Decisive validation: forced both classes onto Paladin's own defense-floor curve while leaving their real combat untouched.** This was the test that actually answered your question above. Substituted the risk-gate's go/no-go check so Ranger (and separately Rogue) used *Paladin's* lethal-hand-fraction lookup for every (mob, HP) pair, but kept simulating real combat outcomes with their own actual cards. Result for Ranger: Gold barely moved (14.5 -> 15.9) but deaths/run exploded 8x (0.167 -> 1.297). Same test on Rogue: Gold moved a little (16.8 -> 18.0), deaths/run went from 0.000 to 1.810. **This proves the caution was correctly reading real, class-specific danger, not an artifact of an overly conservative policy setting** — borrowing Paladin's confidence without also borrowing Paladin's actual defensive capability gets you killed for real. So: no second hidden layer under the binary-gate explanation. It's the real mechanism, confirmed by direct intervention, not just correlation.

**Ranger's fix: Beast Bond: Wolf's base Block, 0 -> 1** (on top of its existing +1/round persistent bonus once active — 2 total Block the round it's played, 1/round after, persistent value itself untouched). A real overcorrection was caught and walked back first: +2 base instead of +1 swung Ranger past Paladin on every metric (Gold 26.2, deaths/run to 0.000, quests/trip 2.30 vs. Paladin's 2.16) — too strong. The final +1 value lands Ranger at Gold 22.6 / quests-per-trip 2.11, just under Paladin's 23.8/2.16 rather than past it. Confirmed the residual gap is now ordinary class variance, not structural: 35.3% of Ranger's 300 trial runs now beat Paladin's own median (was 0.0% before the fix). Notably the fix does *not* close the single worst-case hand at HP=8 vs. Scout (still 1/90 lethal, unchanged) — Beast Bond: Wolf isn't even in that hand. The whole gain comes from the ~67% of hands where it *is* drawn, compounding across a full trip the same way small heal buffs compounded explosively elsewhere in this project (Cleric's Heal, Paladin's own Sacred Light sweep).

**Rogue's fix took two real overcorrections to land right, and turned into a nicer identity story than expected.** Rogue's lethal hand at HP=8 (vs. Ambusher) is literally `Quick Slash, Ambush, Cutthroat, Envenom` — the *only* possible hand missing both of Rogue's Block cards (Dodge/Backstab, Evasion), since only two of six cards carry any Block at all. First attempt: renamed Ambush to "Ambush and Vanish," gave it full evasion (`grants_range`, reusing Wizard/Ranger's mechanic). Closed the worst-case hand outright (0/90 at HP=8) but overshot badly — Gold hit 36.3 against Paladin's 23.8, because full evasion is categorically stronger than incremental Block and the card ended up played in 98.9% of hands where drawn. Reverted. Second attempt: gave Envenom the killing-blow rider Cutthroat already had (mob's attack is prevented entirely if the finisher lands the kill that round) — also closed the worst-case hand (Envenom landing the kill on the problem hand prevents Ambusher's retaliation), still overshot (Gold 31.2) but by less. Kept it, and corrected the overshoot on a different, deliberately macro-level lever instead of reverting: cut Dodge/Backstab's Block 4->2 (renamed to Backstab and Dodge along the way, per a separate, unrelated flavor request) and ROGUE_HP 16->15. Landed at Gold 23.1 / quests-per-trip 2.09, just under Paladin, deaths/run 0.000/300.

Worth flagging directly since it came up mid-session: is cutting HP here a violation of "HP is only the right lever when it's the diagnosed problem"? Argued no, and I think this holds up — the single-pull defense-floor gap already got a real, separately-diagnosed-and-fixed card-level answer (the killing-blow rider). HP is doing a different job afterward: correcting the *aggregate* overshoot that resulted from stacking two independently-justified card buffs (the checked-in killing-blow rider plus the reverted evasion attempt's residual effect), not standing in for an undiagnosed card problem. Chain-level lever fixing a chain-level symptom, not a single-pull-level substitute.

The nicest part of the outcome: Rogue ended up with *less* raw Block (Backstab and Dodge, 4->2) and *less* HP (16->15) than it started with, and performs better anyway, because the entire net gain traces back to one mechanic change. Both finishers now read as "the target doesn't get to hit back if you finish it first" instead of generic durability — a real identity improvement, not just a numbers fix.

**Final locked state, both classes:**
- Ranger: Beast Bond: Wolf base Block 0->1 (persistent bonus unchanged). Gold@24XP 22.6, quests/trip 2.11.
- Rogue: Envenom gains killing-blow rider; Dodge/Backstab renamed Backstab and Dodge, Block 4->2; ROGUE_HP 16->15. Gold@24XP 23.1, quests/trip 2.09.
- Paladin reference: Gold@24XP 23.8, quests/trip 2.16.

Both fully documented in `condensed_ranger.py`/`condensed_rogue.py`'s own docstrings and `CLASS_BALANCE_GUIDE.md`'s "Rogue and Ranger's macro-loop risk outlier" section, including the overcorrections and why they were walked back, not just the final numbers.

If you want to push on anything here, the open thread I'd actually value a second opinion on now: is a residual gap of ~0.7-1.2 Gold / ~0.05-0.07 quests-per-trip (both classes landing just under Paladin rather than exactly matching it) the right place to stop, or should the tuning continue until it's exactly flush? My own instinct is that "just under, with real distribution overlap confirmed" is the correct target — matching Paladin exactly would risk being the same kind of over-fit the two reverted overcorrections were, just smaller — but that's a judgment call, not something the data forces.
